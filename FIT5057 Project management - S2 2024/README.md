## Overview

Applied project management for technology and AI contexts — covering the full project lifecycle from scoping and planning through to delivery, risk governance, and stakeholder communication. The unit treats project management as a structured decision-making discipline rather than administrative overhead, with particular emphasis on how agile and hybrid methodologies adapt to the uncertainty inherent in AI and software development projects.

---

## What I Worked On

- Developed a project charter and scope document for a technology initiative, defining deliverables, constraints, assumptions, and success criteria
- Produced a work breakdown structure (WBS) and activity network, then derived the critical path to identify schedule risk
- Conducted a formal risk register assessment including likelihood/impact scoring and mitigation planning
- Analysed stakeholder maps and communication plans to ensure appropriate information flows across project phases
- Evaluated agile sprint planning and backlog management in the context of iterative AI product development
- Produced a project closure report reflecting on plan vs. actuals and documenting lessons learned

---

## Methods and Approaches

- **Critical Path Method (CPM)** for schedule analysis and float calculation
- **Risk matrix** analysis with quantified likelihood and impact scoring
- **Stakeholder mapping** using power/interest grids to prioritise engagement strategies
- **Agile frameworks** (Scrum, Kanban) applied to iterative, uncertainty-heavy delivery contexts
- **Earned Value Management (EVM)** concepts for tracking schedule and cost performance

---

## Work Breakdown

**Ass 1** → Project initiation and planning deliverable: scope statement, WBS, activity sequencing, and critical path analysis for a defined technology project scenario

**Ass 2** → Risk management plan: risk identification, register construction with quantified scoring, and response strategies aligned to project objectives

**Ass 3** → Reflective project report: evaluation of plan adherence, stakeholder communication effectiveness, and structured lessons-learned analysis

## Key Skills Demonstrated

---

## Key Insights

- The critical path is only as useful as the assumptions underlying task duration estimates — risk management and scheduling are tightly coupled
- Stakeholder misalignment is consistently the highest-impact project risk in AI projects, where technical deliverables are poorly understood by non-technical sponsors
- Agile frameworks reduce delivery risk in uncertain domains but require disciplined backlog prioritisation to avoid scope creep
