## Overview

The mathematical backbone of modern data science and AI — covering linear algebra, multivariable calculus, probability theory, and statistical inference with rigour and depth. The work builds the mathematical fluency that separates practitioners who understand why algorithms work from those who only know how to apply them.

## Key Insights

- Eigendecomposition is not just a linear algebra result — it is the mathematical foundation of PCA, spectral methods, and graph-based learning algorithms; understanding it formally changes how you interpret those tools
- MLE is the conceptual bridge between probability theory and supervised learning: understanding it as an optimisation problem over log-likelihood makes cross-entropy loss self-evident rather than arbitrary
- The chain rule in multivariable calculus is backpropagation — working through it formally demystifies automatic differentiation and makes gradient debugging intuitive
